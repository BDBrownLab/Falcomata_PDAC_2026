### Abstract

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is protected by a dense, fibrotic extracellular matrix (ECM) that sustains an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME). While this protective niche has been described, the molecular determinants orchestrating its formation and dictating its immune interactions remain unclear. Using Perturb-map, a spatial functional genomics platform, we determine how dozens of different gene perturbations shape the growth and cellular environments of PDAC clones through space and time. Our study reveals early gene-specific remodeling of local immune neighborhoods that emerges prior to late-stage clonal consolidation. We identified two plasminogen activator inhibitors - Serpinb2 (PAI2) and Serpine1 (PAI1) - as key orchestrators of TME reprogramming and immune evasion. These tumor-derived factors promote stabilization of a fibrin-rich ECM that spatially anchors macrophages via M2 (CD11b/CD18)-dependent interactions, drives their polarization toward immunosuppressive states, and in turns excludes cytotoxic T cells. Deletion of either Serpinb2 or Serpine1 suppresses tumor growth and sensitizes tumors to anti-PD1 immunotherapy. In  human PDAC, multimodal spatial and single-cell transcriptomic analyses link SERPINB2 and SERPINE1 expression to rare basal-like cancer cell subpopulations that drive spatially dominant immunosuppressive niches, and correlates with poor patient survival. Together, our findings identify cancer cell-derived fibrinolysis regulators as local spatial organizers of immune suppression, linking tumor-intrinsic heterogeneity to local microenvironmental control and therapeutic resistance in PDAC.
